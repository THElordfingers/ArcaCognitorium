Mar 9
Yes, exactly — Textual renders everything through the terminal, so it's entirely bound to whatever font the terminal is using. You can't load a separate typeface for a specific widget. One font, all of it.

But that's not as limiting as it sounds, because you have a few tools that can create the impression of typographic weight:

Unicode character ranges — there are Unicode blocks that contain styled letterforms. Mathematical bold (𝗔𝗕𝗖), mathematical italic (𝐴𝐵𝐶), small caps (ᴀʀᴄᴀ), fullwidth (ＡＲＣＡ). These aren't font changes, they're different characters — so they render in your terminal font but look distinct. The catch is they're only available for the Latin alphabet and digits, and rendering varies.

Figlet/ASCII art text — pyfiglet was ruled out earlier for the header, but for buttons it's probably overkill anyway. For individual words like FILUM or THE COUNCIL, a hand-crafted ASCII treatment could work for the top-level items specifically.

Spacing and border treatment — a lot of the "weight" of a button comes from its surroundings. Generous padding, a distinctive border character, the jewel-tone colour. A plain word centred in a well-constructed frame reads as substantial even in a monospace font.

Your Nerd Font — since you've patched your font, you have access to thousands of icon glyphs that can accompany or frame the button text. That's probably your richest tool.

Realistically the buttons will be your terminal font, but dressed well enough that it doesn't matter. What were you imagining when you pictured "big fancy font"?

It's a font rendering issue — specifically a character width mismatch. In a monospace font every ASCII character is supposed to occupy exactly one cell. But Unicode characters, especially the box-drawing and block element ranges, sometimes get assigned a different cell width by the font metrics than what the terminal actually renders them at.

So when you write:

┌─────┐
│     │
└─────┘
The terminal might render │ at 1 cell but your Nerd Font patched version might be treating certain characters as 1.5 cells or something ambiguous, so the right wall drifts. It compounds across lines.

It's worse with the extended Legacy Computing block characters because those are newer and font support is less standardised. Even within the same font, 🮇 might render at a different effective width than ▎.

The technical term is "ambiguous width" characters — Unicode has a category for characters where the display width is left up to the implementation. Different terminals, different fonts, different decisions.

In Konsole specifically there's a setting for how to handle ambiguous width characters. If it's set to "use Unicode standard" vs "use current locale" you get different results. Worth checking if you haven't already — it can tighten things up considerably.

The practical upshot for the UI is: the most reliable box-drawing is still plain ASCII box characters (┌─┐│└┘) with standard spacing. The fancier stuff is best used as accents and ornaments where pixel-perfect alignment isn't critical rather than as structural frame elements.
